Jay,

Elliman is making a serious investment in AI with Elli and Elius. The hard part will not be building the tools. It will be getting agents to use them.

The Elliman Way to AIThe Adoption Problem

The Adoption Problem

Companies buy the tools. People don't use them. A decade of research from the largest firms in the world says the same thing.

SourceYearFinding
Accenture with CSO Insights201680% of companies have a CRM; only 13% of salespeople use its full capabilities. 59% say they must use too many tools.
Microsoft & LinkedIn Work Trend Index202475% of knowledge workers use AI at work. 78% of them bring their own AI tools.
Gartner Digital Worker Survey2024Only 23% of employees are completely satisfied with their work applications, down from 30% in 2022.
Gartner2024Only 48% of digital initiatives meet their business targets.
WalkMe (SAP), State of Digital Adoption2025Enterprises wasted $104 million on underused technology in 2024. Only 28% of employees know how to use their company's AI tools.
MIT NANDA, The GenAI Divide202595% of enterprise AI pilots showed no measurable profit impact. Workers at over 90% of firms use personal AI tools anyway.

The pattern: people already use AI. They use their own.

Real Estate Is No Exception

Agents are independent contractors. They pick their own tools, and no one can require them to change.

  • Compass, 2026. A live demo day, CEO office roadshows and in-person training. Within weeks, about 15,000 of 83,000 agents had used its AI Assistant, roughly 18%.
"It takes a lot of work to get agents to actually adopt these tools."Rory Golod, President of Growth, Compass · Fortune, September 2026
  • Keller Williams Command. Agents told RealTrends they used it mainly because they had to register sales in it. One called it "clunky" for running a team.
  • The counter-example: Morgan Stanley. Its AI assistant reached about 98% of advisor teams because it met advisors inside tools they already used.

The lesson: adoption follows familiarity. Meet people where they already work.

What's in the Book

  1. What changed in AI this year, in plain language, and what it means for a listing, a buyer and a closing.
  2. AI skills and commands: reusable instructions that make any AI work the same way every time.
  3. A prompt library for an agent's daily work: CMAs, listing presentations, follow-up, market updates, client email and contract review.
  4. The Elliman standard: brand voice, Fair Housing guardrails, and what client data never goes into a public AI tool.
  5. The bridge to Elli and Elius: when to move a task from a general AI into Elliman's platform, step by step.

Every prompt is written by an Elliman agent and tested on real listings and real clients.
